# An open Doom deck that nobody touches works at most 150 ms in 3 s and runs no long task over 50 ms, read over a quiet span the test does not drive

`tests/browser/doom-speed.spec.ts` reads the renderer's `TaskDuration` in thread time before and after a kept 3 s wait, and fails at 150 ms. Before the wait starts, it installs a buffered `longtask` `PerformanceObserver`, and fails after the wait if any task that started inside the window ran over 50 ms. The test sends no frame of its own in that span, so it reads only what the deck schedules: timers, animation frames, idle callbacks, observers, and the frames they cause.

The 150 ms total budget alone misses a single slow periodic task. In the 2026-11-23 prototype, a planted 60 ms task every 2.5 s read 66.0, 67.5, and 69.0 ms total with fixed phase and 65.6 and 68.2 ms with random phase, passing the 150 ms total budget in all 5 runs even though a 60 ms task is a visible stutter. Adding the 50 ms long-task check failed that random-phase plant in 2 of 2 runs (74.3 ms total with a 61 ms task, and 67.7 ms total with a 56 ms task). On a clean 40-slide deck, the combined check read 10.5 and 10.6 ms alone, 9.3 to 10.5 ms across 3 parallel runs, and 12.1 and 17.0 ms under 6x CPU throttling, with 0 long tasks in every run. A planted 60 ms task every 1 s failed both checks at 177.9 ms total and 3 long tasks (longest 56 ms).

Shortening the wait, fake time, and virtual time were tried in the same prototype and rejected. A 1 s real wait with a 50 ms budget passed the random-phase 60 ms every 2.5 s plant in 4 of 6 runs (2.0 to 2.3 ms when the period missed the 1 s window, 58.1 and 63.3 ms when it hit) and passed the fixed-phase plant in 1 of 4 runs (47.9 ms). Playwright's `page.clock` fake time advanced 3000 ms in about 20 ms of wall time without driving frames, CSS animations, or observers, so it passed a `requestAnimationFrame` slide-move loop (15.2 ms, 2 layouts), a CSS width animation (5.3 and 5.4 ms, 1 layout, 17 ms animation advance), and a `ResizeObserver` loop (1.1 ms, 0 layouts). DevTools virtual time (`Emulation.setVirtualTimePolicy`) advanced 3000 ms in 14 to 23 ms of wall time and missed the same loops: `requestAnimationFrame` read 0.9 and 5.8 ms (0 to 1 layouts), the CSS width animation read 1.0 ms (0 layouts), the `ResizeObserver` loop read 0.9 and 1.0 ms (0 layouts), and even a 2 ms timer every 16 ms passed at 148.4 ms. Over a real 3 s quiet span, those same plants all failed: 420.5 and 422.4 ms for the 2 ms every 16 ms timer, 723.1 and 783.2 ms (180 layouts) for `requestAnimationFrame`, 737.7 and 770.2 ms (183 to 186 layouts) for the CSS animation, and 763.9 and 771.2 ms (180 layouts) for `ResizeObserver`.

Until 2026-11-23 the span was 180 animation frames the test requested itself. Each frame ran a full frame of the 40-slide editor, about 1 ms of style, intersection, and commit work, so the reading was mostly the test's own frames: 149 to 154 ms with three runs at once, and 524 to 618 ms under 6x CPU throttling.
