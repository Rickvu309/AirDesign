# Checks on one device's undo and redo stacks

The entry has the same parts as those in [checks-doom.md](checks-doom.md).

## undo-steps

One device's undo and redo stacks, as `turn` in `packages/live/src/doom-deck.ts` keeps them, over three properties on two layers: the title's text (`T`), the title's parent (`P`: standing, deleted or not made yet) and the card's box (`C`). The text and the box are 0 at the start. When the title is in the file, the person nudges the card to 1, then makes a step that writes 2 to the text, the box or both in one edit, or types 2 in the title and deletes it in the same edit, as leaving a text box emptied does (`leaveEmptyWrites`). When it is not, the person makes one step that makes the title with text 1 and nudges the card to 1. A push drops a write that would not change its property and makes no step when none is left (`changingWrites`). Once the person has a step, and before the first press, another tab may write the text or the box once, to 1 or 2, so its value is free to equal one the person left. Once the person has a step, it may delete the title once, at any time the title stands. A deleted layer keeps its properties, but the deck refuses any write to them (`missing`) and refuses the whole edit with it. An undo looks at the top step and puts back what it replaced on each property whose last writer is the step or a turn that landed (`invert`) and whose layer still stands (`onStandingLayers` in `packages/doom/src/standing-layers.ts`: a layer stands unless its parent is `null` and the undo does not put it back, and a move into a deleted frame, a frame inside one, or the layer itself is left out the same way, and so are the other writes to a layer whose move is left out and the delete of a frame that would still hold a layer; undo-moves follows those moves and undo-frames those deletes). Of a layer the step made, it puts back only the parent, which deletes it. It writes a move that puts a layer back first and a delete last, so it never writes to a layer it deletes. With nothing left it takes the step off and answers `nothing`; a refused turn keeps its step on top; otherwise it lands, takes the step off, and pushes the step that puts it back on the other stack. Up to three presses.

This copies Figma: an undo of your edit to a layer someone else deleted does nothing, and the next undo reaches the step below (Liveblocks, "How to build undo/redo in a multiplayer environment": "Figma and Google Slides both handle this the same way — nothing happens"; Figma's own post, figma.com/blog/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works, leaves undo across writers open and keeps a deleted object's properties only in the deleting client's undo buffer).

- `NeverStuck`: every press takes a step off its stack, so the next press reaches the step below. Harm if broken: another tab deletes the layer the top step wrote, every Ctrl+Z is refused `missing` for good, and no step below can be undone (verdict 1494-0ca0e9e, finding 5). Or the person empties a text box and leaves it, which deletes it in the same step as the typing: after Ctrl+Z, every Ctrl+Shift+Z is refused `missing` with no notice, and no step above can be redone (verdict 1507-1fb8aa5, finding 1).
- `NeverLosesAStep`: a press that answers nothing takes off only a step nothing of which still shows: each property it wrote is on a deleted layer, or the other tab or a step still waiting on a stack wrote it last. A turn that lands puts back what the step replaced on every property it names. Harm if broken: a step that also nudged a layer still on the slide is dropped whole with the deleted one.
- `NeverCoversTheirs`: a turn never writes over a property the other tab wrote last, whatever values either left. Harm if broken: Ctrl+Z covers another person's newer change.
- The replay also reads, at every edge, the three values, the edit that wrote each last and what the press answered from the real device deck, and that the server holds what the tab shows.

Bounds: two steps before the first press when the title is in the file and one when the person makes it, the other tab's write before the first press, its delete at any time after the person's first step, three presses. 485 states. Run: `pnpm check:specs undo-steps`. Replay: `tests/undo-steps-replay.test.ts` drives all 512 edges from both starts through the real `createDoomDeck`, `openDoomDeck` and `memoryDoomRemote`, the person's tab and another tab on one server: each edge replays the shortest path to its source on a fresh deck, then the edge. The person's step pushes the changed properties plus each standing property that already holds the step's value, so `changingWrites` drops the latter; it writes a new title's parent first and a delete last, as the editor does. `packages/doom/src/invert.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-deleted-layer.test.ts` and `tests/browser/doom-undo-deleted-layer.spec.ts` hold the cases beside it: a move out of a frame another tab deleted, a redo on a deleted layer, a step that deleted its own layer, a text box emptied and left then undone and redone, and two real tabs.

Leaves out: a refusal other than `missing`, such as `last-slide`, which keeps its step so the undo works once the deck allows it (`doom-undo-steps.test.ts`); moves between nested frames, which undo-moves follows; an undo queued offline over a layer another tab deletes before the tab reconnects, which `drain` drops whole when it is refused `missing`, so the step below is lost too (verdict 1507-1fb8aa5, finding 2, still open); the other tab putting the layer back after the press took the step off (as in Figma, the step stays gone); a write to a layer inside a deleted group, which the deck does not refuse, so its undo lands where no one sees it (verdict 1507-1fb8aa5, finding 4, still open); the network and the queue (each action syncs before the next; doom models the queue); typing steps of several flushes (`doom-undo-unchanged.test.ts`, `doom-undo-chain.test.ts`); agent runs and their undo (doom-runs).

## undo-moves

One device's undo and redo stacks over moves between nested frames. The slide holds frames F and H, F holds G, and G holds J. Before the first press the person makes two steps, each dragging G or J, while it shows, into G, J or H when that frame shows and is not inside it, as the editor lets a drag drop it. Once the person has a step, another tab may act once, at any time: delete F or H, or drag G or J the same way. An undo looks at the top step. When the layer's parent was last written by the step or a turn that landed, it puts back the parent the step replaced, if that frame and every frame above it stand and it is not inside the layer (`onStandingLayers` in `packages/doom/src/standing-layers.ts`). Otherwise it takes the step off and answers `nothing`. A turn that lands pushes the step that moves the layer back on the other stack. Up to three presses.

This copies Figma for a frame someone deleted, as in undo-steps. For a frame that now sits inside the layer Figma has no rule we know of; leaving the layer where it is keeps every step below reachable, as for a frame that is gone.

- `NeverStuck`: every press takes a step off its stack. Harm if broken: the person drags J from G into H and G into J, another tab deletes F, which held G, and after the first Ctrl+Z answers nothing, every Ctrl+Z is refused `cycle` for good (verdict 1510-559d9e2, finding 3). Or another tab drags G into J after the person dragged J out of G, and every Ctrl+Z is refused.
- `NeverLosesAStep`: a press that answers nothing takes off only a spent step: someone else moved its layer since, or the frame it came from is gone or now sits inside the layer. A turn that lands puts the layer back where the step found it, and the layer shows there. Harm if broken: the person drags J out of G, another tab deletes F, which holds G, and Ctrl+Z puts J back inside the deleted frame, where it vanishes.
- `NeverCoversTheirs`: a turn never moves a layer the other tab moved last.
- The replay reads, at every edge, both parents, the edit that wrote each last, which frame is deleted and what the press answered from the real device deck, and that the server holds what the tab shows.

Bounds: two single-move steps before the first press, one action by the other tab at any time after the person's first step, three presses, G, J and H as drop targets. 402 states. Run: `pnpm check:specs undo-moves`. Replay: `tests/undo-moves-replay.test.ts` drives all 401 edges through the real `createDoomDeck`, `openDoomDeck` and `memoryDoomRemote`, the person's tab and another tab on one server: each edge replays the shortest path to its source on a fresh deck, then the edge. `packages/doom/src/invert.test.ts`, `packages/doom/src/invert-run.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-deleted-layer.test.ts` and `packages/live/src/doom-undo-steps.test.ts` hold the cases beside it: a step that moves two layers, whose second move still lands when the first is left out, and the same rule for an agent run's undo.

Leaves out: a step that moves several layers in one edit, and the order `invert` writes them in (`invert.test.ts`); a step that makes or deletes a frame, which undo-frames follows; the slide and F as drop targets, which only widen the same paths; property writes (undo-steps); an undo queued offline (verdict 1507-1fb8aa5, finding 2, still open); agent runs (doom-runs; `invert-run.test.ts` holds the same rule for a run's undo).
