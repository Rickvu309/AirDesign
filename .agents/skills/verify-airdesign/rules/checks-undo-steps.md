# Checks on one device's undo and redo stacks

The entry has the same parts as those in [checks-doom.md](checks-doom.md).

## undo-steps

One device's undo and redo stacks, as `turn` in `packages/live/src/doom-deck.ts` keeps them, over two properties on two layers: the title's text (`T`) and the card's box (`C`), both 0 at the start. The person nudges the card to 1, then makes a step that writes 2 to the text, the box or both in one edit. A push drops a write that would not change its property and makes no step when none is left (`changingWrites`). Once the person has a step, and before the first press, another tab may write either property once, to 1 or 2, so its value is free to equal one the person left; it may delete the title once, at any time. A deleted layer keeps its properties, but the deck refuses any write to them (`missing`). An undo looks at the top step and puts back what it replaced on each property whose last writer is the step or a turn that landed (`invert`) and whose layer still stands (`onStandingLayers` in `packages/doom/src/invert.ts`: a layer stands unless its parent is `null` and the undo does not put it back, and a move into a deleted layer is left out the same way). With nothing left it takes the step off and answers `nothing`; otherwise it lands, takes the step off, and pushes the step that puts it back on the other stack. Up to three presses.

This copies Figma: an undo of your edit to a layer someone else deleted does nothing, and the next undo reaches the step below (Liveblocks, "How to build undo/redo in a multiplayer environment": "Figma and Google Slides both handle this the same way — nothing happens"; Figma's own post, figma.com/blog/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works, leaves undo across writers open and keeps a deleted object's properties only in the deleting client's undo buffer).

- `NeverStuck`: every press takes a step off its stack, so the next press reaches the step below. Harm if broken: another tab deletes the layer the top step wrote, every Ctrl+Z is refused `missing` for good, and no step below can be undone (verdict 1494-0ca0e9e, finding 5).
- `NeverLosesAStep`: a press that answers nothing takes off only a step nothing of which still shows: each property it wrote is on a deleted layer, or the other tab or a step still waiting on a stack wrote it last. A turn that lands puts back what the step replaced on every property it names. Harm if broken: a step that also nudged a layer still on the slide is dropped whole with the deleted one.
- `NeverCoversTheirs`: a turn never writes over a property the other tab wrote last, whatever values either left. Harm if broken: Ctrl+Z covers another person's newer change.
- The replay also reads, at every edge, both values, the edit that wrote each last, whether the title stands and what the press answered from the real device deck, and that the server holds what the tab shows.

Bounds: two steps before the first press, the other tab's write before the first press, its delete at any time, three presses. 399 states. Run: `pnpm check:specs undo-steps`. Replay: `tests/undo-steps-replay.test.ts` drives all 417 edges from the start through the real `createDoomDeck`, `openDoomDeck` and `memoryDoomRemote`, the person's tab and another tab on one server: each edge replays the shortest path to its source on a fresh deck, then the edge. The person's step pushes the changed properties plus each standing property that already holds the step's value, so `changingWrites` drops the latter. `packages/doom/src/invert.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-deleted-layer.test.ts` and `tests/browser/doom-undo-deleted-layer.spec.ts` hold the cases beside it: a move out of a frame another tab deleted, a redo on a deleted layer, a step that deleted its own layer, and two real tabs.

Leaves out: a refusal other than `missing`, such as `cycle`, which keeps its step so the undo works once the deck allows it (`doom-undo-steps.test.ts`); the other tab putting the layer back after the press took the step off (as in Figma, the step stays gone); a layer inside a deleted group, which the deck does not refuse, so its undo lands where no one sees it; the network and the queue (each action syncs before the next; doom models the queue); typing steps of several flushes (`doom-undo-unchanged.test.ts`, `doom-undo-chain.test.ts`); agent runs and their undo (doom-runs).
