# Pitch or keynote

A speaker carries the words and the slides carry the picture. Each slide gives the room one thing to look at while the speaker talks, and every slide moves the room toward the ask.

Work freely. At each pause point, stop and confirm every item. Fix what fails before you go on.

## Read on these triggers

- Writing or rewriting titles: `principles/headlines`.
- A slide shows a number or a chart: `principles/number-truth`.
- Choosing the pillars of the talk, or ordering a list: `principles/one-two-three`.
- The pitch asks for money or people to carry out a plan: `principles/strategy-5w1h`.

## Pieces that fit this job

Options, chosen by the moment in the talk. Copy them from `kit`; each recipe is a script you run.

- The opening and section breaks: `kit/cover`, `kit/statement`.
- One number the room remembers: `kit/kpi` or a lone `bigNumber`, with what it compares to.
- A share of a market or a whole: `kit/share-box`, one bar in place of a pie.
- Growth over time: `charts/column` or `charts/line`. Ranked items: `charts/bar`.
- What comes next: `kit/timeline-step`. Where the talk is: `kit/section-tracker`.
- Two ways compared, ours and theirs: `kit/comparison`.

## Pause 1: Setup

- [ ] The mode is `live`. Builds are fine when the speaker controls the flow and the room does not interrupt.
- [ ] The tone (confident, or optimistic with named dependencies) and the angle for this audience are chosen.
- [ ] The talk has three pillars of equal rank, previewed early and recapped at the end.
- [ ] The slide count fits the slot: about one slide per minute or two of talk.

## Pause 2: Content

- [ ] Every slide sells something: the ask, a belief to change, or the ground for a later ask.
- [ ] Each big number is made human: per person, per day, against something familiar, before and after, or the cost of waiting.
- [ ] When raw size is small, the case rests on growth or efficiency, and the raw figure still appears.
- [ ] Each slide holds one idea and at most three short points; the speaker says the rest.

## Pause 3: Headlines

- [ ] Each title is a short hook the room gets at a glance, and its figure sits on the same slide.
- [ ] Titles state the story, not the metric: "Customers stay twice as long", not "Retention by cohort".
- [ ] The closing slide restates the ask with a date or a next step.

## Pause 4: Visuals

- [ ] One focal element per slide, in the accent; everything else steps back.
- [ ] Shares show as sorted bars or a share bar. Every shape is flat and two-dimensional.
- [ ] A picture or icon earns its place by saying what a word cannot.
- [ ] Text reads from the back of the room: the body at `body` or larger.
