# Decision deck

The reader is a decider with little time, and the deck exists to get one yes. Assume the meeting collapses: they arrive late, the slot is cut, they read only the first slide and the titles. The deck still wins on those alone.

Work freely. At each pause point, stop and confirm every item. Fix what fails before you go on.

## Pieces that fit this job

Options, chosen by what the slide must prove. Copy them from `kit`; each recipe is a script you run.

- The answer up front: `kit/statement`, or `kit/title-block` over the evidence.
- The thesis as a product of its drivers, each with its state and its implication: `kit/driver-equation`.
- A recommendation that holds only under conditions: `kit/two-by-two`, quadrants named by the action each implies.
- Options side by side: `kit/comparison` for two or three, `charts/table` with the recommended row boxed for more attributes.
- Money from a start to an end: `charts/waterfall`. Ranked items: `charts/bar`. Periods: `charts/column` or `charts/stacked-column`.
- Proof that an action caused a result: `charts/line`, the treated group in the accent against a matched control in gray, the start marked.
- The open questions behind the recommendation, each with its hypothesis, the analysis that tests it and its owner: `kit/qa-plan-table`.
- Headline figures: `kit/kpi`. Takeaways tied to one exhibit: `kit/numbered-callout`. Sources: `kit/source-line`.

## Pause 1: Setup

- [ ] The ask is one sentence, and slide 1 or 2 says a decision is needed today.
- [ ] The plan is 5 to 8 slides with the cover, with no appendix, divider or recap slide unless the person asked. Each ask or finding keeps the slide with its evidence. The model's detail sits in a table on the slide it proves.
- [ ] The lead argument fits the decider's function: return for finance, market and customers for product, risk for operations.
- [ ] The first content slide alone carries the answer and the ask. No later slide says the recommendation again.

## Pause 2: Content

- [ ] The thesis fits one if-then: if we do X (and not W), Y follows, because Z. Everything else is evidence for it.
- [ ] Reasons come in three of equal rank, with risks and their mitigations as one of them when the decider will ask.
- [ ] Options are ordered by impact, the recommended one named, and rejected options listed with the reason for each.
- [ ] The ask names owners, money, sign-offs and dates, and the assumptions that must hold.
- [ ] No body runs over five points or about 40 words.
- [ ] The material's own model is tested before the deck relies on it. Does an input already include the effect it is said to add? Divide totals by counts to find averages and caps the plan must respect. A gap you print must reproduce from the material's own numbers; when your rebuild disagrees, look for the factor you left out before you claim a gap. Does a cap, limit or segment size in the material bound the claimed effect? Do the parts behave as the plan assumes? Each answer, with its arithmetic, sits on the slide beside the figure it tests.
- [ ] Every total is re-added from its parts, and every percentage and difference recomputed, before it goes on a slide. Where the material's own figure disagrees, the slide shows the recomputed one, and the gap, both figures named, sits under the exhibit it changes.

## Pause 3: Headlines

- [ ] Each title is one sentence of 12 words or fewer, two lines at most.
- [ ] Read alone, the titles go: recommendation, reasons, ask.
- [ ] Each title gives a verdict or a move. A cautious title names what it depends on.
- [ ] The last slide is the ask: what to approve, owners, money and dates.
- [ ] Every number, date and name in a title matches the material exactly (£4.8M stays £4.8M) or, for a total you re-added, your figure with the material's named on the slide, and a date or deadline appears only when the material gives it or one step of arithmetic on it does.

## Pause 4: Visuals

- [ ] Each exhibit proves its own title, and one element per exhibit carries the accent.
- [ ] Every causal title has its control comparison on the slide.
- [ ] Every number has its comparator and a source line.
- [ ] Builds and animations: none. One slide with numbered callouts replaces a highlight sequence.
