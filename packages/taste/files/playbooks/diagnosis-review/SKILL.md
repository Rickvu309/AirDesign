# Diagnosis review

The reader wants an honest account: what moved, why it moved, and what happens next. Reviews travel, so the deck makes its case read alone, from the titles and the first slide.

Work freely. At each pause point, stop and confirm every item. Fix what fails before you go on.

## Read on these triggers

- Read alone in order, the titles fail to tell the story: `principles/headlines`.
- A re-added total or a recomputed figure disagrees with the material, or a title claims a cause: `principles/number-truth`.
- The results, causes or next steps will not fall into three of equal rank: `principles/one-two-three`.
- The review ends in a plan with several levers, owners and dates: `principles/strategy-5w1h`.

## Pieces that fit this job

Options, chosen by what the slide must prove. Copy them from `kit`; each recipe is a script you run.

- Results against target: `kit/kpi`, the target beside each actual.
- A result over periods: `charts/column`. A change in mix under a flat total: `charts/stacked-column`, the breaking period boxed and annotated.
- What moved a number from one period to the next: `charts/waterfall`.
- Cause and effect: `charts/line`, the treated group in the accent against a matched control in gray, the intervention marked.
- Causes laid out: `diagrams/issue-tree`, left to right, the path pursued in the accent and the rest in gray. Causes still open, with the analysis that will test each and its owner: `kit/qa-plan-table`.
- Segments ranked: `charts/bar`. Entities with their metrics: `charts/table`, grouped by segment, the focus rows marked.
- Takeaways tied to one exhibit: `kit/numbered-callout`. Sections: `kit/section-tracker`. Sources: `kit/source-line`.

## Pause 1: Setup

- [ ] Slide 1 or 2 says what kind of session this is (inform, or solve a problem) and what the reader must do, if anything.
- [ ] The plan is 5 to 8 slides with the cover, with no appendix, divider or recap slide unless the person asked. Detail sits in a table on the slide that needs it. Each ask or finding keeps the slide with its evidence.
- [ ] A reader new to the topic gets one context slide, and every acronym is spelled out once.
- [ ] Every point reads alone: a subject and a claim, never a teaser.

## Pause 2: Content

- [ ] The break in the pattern has a title: the dip after steady growth, the lagging segment, the mix shift under a flat total.
- [ ] Segments go down to the break and stop before samples too small to act on; each cut shows its sample size.
- [ ] Causes come from a tree whose first split leaves no third option, and no cause sits in two branches. Each cause is marked proven, disproven or open, with its evidence.
- [ ] A causal claim has a matched group that held flat, and a lift bigger than the metric's normal swing (1 to 1.5 standard deviations). Otherwise it reads as directional.
- [ ] Each result sits beside a like-for-like benchmark, the market's rate or its own history.
- [ ] Credit for a lagging metric goes to the work that drove it, not to a new initiative.
- [ ] The material's own model is tested before the deck relies on it: rebuild each modeled figure from its stated inputs, and a factor left over is an effect already inside it. Divide totals by counts to find averages the plan must respect. Does a cap, limit or segment size in the material bound the claimed effect? Do the parts behave as the plan assumes? Each answer, with its arithmetic, sits on the slide beside the figure it tests.
- [ ] Every total is re-added from its parts, and every percentage and difference recomputed, before it goes on a slide. Where the material's own figure disagrees, the slide shows the recomputed one, and the gap, both figures named, sits under the exhibit it changes.

## Pause 3: Headlines

- [ ] Each title is one sentence of 12 words or fewer, two lines at most.
- [ ] Read alone, the titles tell the story: result, break, cause, next step.
- [ ] Misses appear in titles as plainly as wins, in one tone across the deck.
- [ ] Each title passes the obviousness test: a reader could disagree with it.
- [ ] Every number, date and name in a title matches the material exactly: £4.8M stays £4.8M, and a date or deadline appears only when the material gives it or one step of arithmetic on it does.
- [ ] The last slide is the next steps, with owners and dates.

## Pause 4: Visuals

- [ ] A test-versus-control result is a `charts/line` with the intervention marked. It replaces a table of the same numbers.
- [ ] Every colored hit or miss shows its target beside it. Green means progress, amber short of goal, red risk.
- [ ] One element per exhibit carries the accent, and it is the one the title names.
- [ ] Each exhibit proves its own title, and each slide with outside or derived numbers has a source line.
