# Number truth

A wrong number ends the reader's trust in every other number. Each figure on a slide traces to the material, and each one means something to the reader.

## Exact

- Every number comes from the material, or from one step of arithmetic on it. Write the arithmetic down before you type the result.
- Round once, from the source value. 108,475 €k becomes €108.5m; rounding a rounded number drifts.
- One precision across a series: all whole numbers, or one decimal when the values are under 10.
- One metric has one value across the whole deck.
- A group like "Others" is the sum of its members, computed, never estimated.
- When the material's parts do not add up to its total, show the parts and say so in a note. Never repeat a wrong total.

## Meaningful

- Every headline number has a comparator on the same slide: target, prior period, peer or market.
- A change shows both ends, or the change itself: "from 168 to 212 robots", "+26%".
- A giant total also gets a human scale: per customer, per day, or a share of the whole.
- Size shows by comparison. An adjective such as huge or record stands only next to its comparator.
- A figure earns its place when it could change what the reader decides.

## Honest

- A claim that X caused Y has evidence for the cause on its slide. Without it, say "came with" rather than "drove".
- A forecast is labelled an estimate, shown as a range, and names the assumptions it rests on.
- A hit or miss in color shows its target beside it.
- A smaller yardstick may lead the slide, and the raw figure still appears.

## Drawn

- Bars and columns start at zero; the longest takes the full track.
- A target line carries its label, set apart from the bar ends.
- The unit is named once, in the header or column head: $M, %, FTE. Values carry it when they stand alone.
- Labels sit on or beside their marks, so a single series needs no legend.
- Every slide with outside numbers has a source line at the bottom left. A derived metric gets a footnote that defines it.
