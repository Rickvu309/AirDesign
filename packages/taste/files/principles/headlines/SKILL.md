# Headlines

The titles carry the deck. A reader who reads only the titles, in order, gets the whole argument.

## Write them first

Write every title, in order, before you build any slide body: a ghost deck. Then build each body to prove its title.

## One title

- **A claim, with a verb.** It says what happened and whether that is good or bad, or what to do about it. What the data is goes in a small caption under the title: "Bookings by quarter, $M".
- **Outcome first.** The number or result sits in the first half: "Churn fell to 2% after the onboarding change", not "After the onboarding change, churn fell to 2%".
- **The story, not the metric.** "Customers are leaving" beats "Retention trended down for three quarters".
- **Plain words, one clause,** at most one "but" or "so". Replace leverage, alignment, robust, momentum, headroom and key with the specific claim they stand for.
- **Disputable.** If no reader could disagree, the title is wasted. "Teamwork matters" fails; "Two-person teams shipped 30% faster" holds.
- **Length by mode.** Read-alone: a full claim of 12 words or fewer. Live: a short hook the room gets at a glance, its figure on the same slide.
- **Honest tone.** A miss reads as plainly as a win. A cautious title names what it depends on: "Margin reaches 40% if the supplier deal closes".

| Label         | Claim                                                    |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Q3 revenue    | Revenue beat plan by 8% on two new markets               |
| Hiring update | Hiring is 8 people behind plan, slowing enterprise deals |
| Architecture  | One queue between ingest and render halves p99 latency   |

## The deck's titles

1. **Titles-only test.** Read the titles as one paragraph. It flows with no gap, jump or repeat, and ends on the ask.
2. **Answer first.** The first content title states the conclusion; the evidence follows it.
3. **Support test.** Everything on a slide backs its title. A title with no evidence on its slide moves, changes or goes.
4. **One point each.** Two titles that say the same thing become one slide.
5. After any reorder, run tests 1 and 3 again.
