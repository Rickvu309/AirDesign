# An undo never hides a layer that shows, never drops a visible edit to a layer it leaves in place, never loses a deleted layer whose frames come back, never deletes a frame that still holds a layer, and never moves a layer by the numbers of a frame it has left or into a frame by the numbers of another

`onStandingLayers` in `packages/doom/src/standing-layers.ts` is the one judge of what an undo may put back, for a person's step (`invert` in `packages/doom/src/invert.ts`) and for an agent's run (`invertRun` in `packages/doom/src/invert-run.ts`). It leaves out every write to a layer whose parent is `null` unless the same undo puts that layer back, a move into a deleted frame or into the layer itself, and, while the layer shows, a move into a frame under a deleted one, so Ctrl+Z never makes a layer vanish into a frame someone else deleted. A layer that does not show, because it is deleted or sits under a deleted frame, goes back into its old frame even under a deleted one, and comes back when that frame does: delete a card in Outer > Inner, another tab deletes Outer, and Ctrl+Z puts the card back into Inner, so their Ctrl+Z brings Outer, Inner and the card back (verdict 1514-a78b019, finding 2). Strict Figma would lose the card there, since their delete took Inner with Outer and an undo into a deleted frame does nothing; AirDesign keeps it, as `main` did. A deleted layer goes back even into the deleted frame itself: `write` in `packages/doom/src/apply-edit.ts` lands a move into a deleted frame only for a write that takes back a delete (`takesBackDelete`), one that names the edit it replaces as its `base`, as every undo, redo and run undo does, of a layer deleted before the edit. The layer stays hidden there and comes back with the frame, so a card deleted from Inner while another tab deletes Inner comes back when they bring Inner back (verdict 1514-d30cef9, finding 6). Every other move there is refused `missing`: an ordinary move, such as a reorder sent offline or from a tab that has not caught up after another tab deleted the layer and then its frame, the move of a layer that shows or is only hidden, and a new layer, even one the same edit deleted first. When that reorder landed, their Ctrl+Z on the frame brought the layer back too, and their next Ctrl+Z did nothing (verdict 1533-b022c58, findings 1 and 2). `NeverLosesALayer` in `UndoDeletedFrames.tla` fails in 56 states when the deck refuses that restore, as `main` did, and `NeverLosesAStep` in 30 when any move of K into a deleted frame lands; its replay fails 10 of 473 edges and `tests/undo-frames-replay.test.ts` 7 of 497 on the old refusal. `specs/undo-pending-moves/UndoPendingMoves.tla` fails `OneStepAtATime` in 96 states when any write may move a deleted layer into a deleted frame (b022c58), and `NeverLosesALayer` in 58 when no write may (`main`); `tests/undo-pending-moves-replay.test.ts` fails 8 of 498 edges on the first and 51 on the second. For a layer whose move it leaves out, it leaves out only where the layer sits in its frame: `x`, `y` and `rotation` of `box` (`boxLeftInPlace`), and both ends of a line's `geometry` (`lineLeftInPlace`), attached or free, which are measured in the frame. The line keeps the ends it has now, as `main` does, so an undo never moves a line end. Giving an attached end back to its shape when the shape draws it where the end sits now is out of scope: the painter measures text-sized shapes, so the judge would need the painter's measure in `invert`, and without it an end can jump (verdict 1532-150e42f, M1, 35 px). Four rounds of reattaching failed (verdicts 1532-244c214, 595289e, de802c6, 150e42f) and it saved 7 of 102 attachments, so it is a follow-up on its own. The live tests in `packages/live/src/doom-undo-line-ends-stay.test.ts` hold every case that failed: a shape deleted, hidden, under a hidden frame, moved, on another slide, or sized by its words. A line whose attached shape is hidden or moved off its slide with no undo at all draws that end at its frame's corner already, on `main`, and is a separate fault. A frame turns what it holds but never mirrors it, since `packages/render/src/node-art.tsx` flips only a layer's own body, so the flips come back. A layer with no stored `box` had the empty box at its frame's corner, as `solveLayout` reads it, so the undo puts that size back where the layer sits (finding 4). The undo takes only the step's own change of the layer's turn off the turn it has now (`turnsLeftInPlace`), measured on the slide as the step left the layer against before the step, and sets the turn in its frame so that, with the frames as the undo leaves them, the layer ends at that turn on the slide. So a turn the step gave the layer, such as an agent's own `rotation` or the turn an agent's `move` gives a layer by taking it out of a turned frame, comes back off (finding 2), while every turn another tab gave the layer's frames since stays on it. A person's step measures from the deck the step started on; an agent run from the deck the device saw it start on (`watched` in `packages/live/src/doom-deck.ts`), and after a reload, with no such deck, the layer keeps its turn as it did before. An agent's `undo_run` (`packages/mcp/src/undo-tool.ts`) has no device, so it reads the layer positions from the edit history before the run (`positionsAt` in `DeckHost`, `runPositions` in the API) and passes those positions, so agents get the same turn rule; when those positions are unavailable it keeps the turn, as `main` did. Two rules that rebuilt the turn from the old and new frames failed: one measured the frames as they stand at the undo, so it cancelled another tab's later turn (verdict 1532-759e007, finding 1), and one assumed the layer still had the turn the step left it in its frame, so a Ctrl+Z after Undo on an agent run turned it away from where the person saw it (verdict 1532-cfcc610, finding 1). Every other write still lands, the layer's size among them, so an agent run that moved a card out of a frame since deleted and painted it red, retyped it or resized it gets its colour, words and size back (verdict 1514-a78b019, findings 1 and 3). It treats a layer the same way when an undo writes its box but not its parent and the layer no longer sits in the frame its box was measured in, the frame it sat in before the step (`strayedFrom`; `invert` reads it from the deck before the step and `invertRun` from the parent each of the run's edits replaced): the step's `x` and `y` are that frame's numbers, so `x`, `y` and both of a line's ends stay, the turn loses only the step's own change, and the size and the flips come back, so a later Ctrl+Z on a resize never sends a layer an agent run moved out of its frame across the slide (verdict 1532-cfcc610, finding 1). The frame the step left it in is the wrong frame to compare: when the step moved the layer, another writer then rewrote its parent to the same frame, the undo cannot write the parent back, and the box it writes is still the old frame's (verdict 1542-c5ba7cd, finding 1). What it keeps is the layer's place, so the step's own nudge is not undone there; taking the step's offset off the place the layer has now is not done. `PutsBackWhereSeen` in `specs/undo-strays/UndoStrays.tla` fails in 50 states without it, its replay `tests/undo-strays-replay.test.ts` fails 55 of 342 edges on c5ba7cd's rule, and `packages/live/src/doom-undo-stray-box.test.ts` and `tests/browser/editor-undo-deleted-layer.spec.ts` fail on `main`. The mirror is an undo that writes a layer's parent and not its box, because someone wrote the box after the step began: the box the layer has is measured in the frame it sits in, so putting the parent back alone moves it by the offset of the two frames, a run that moved a card out of a frame, the person's nudge, then Undo on the run sent the card 100 pixels across the slide (verdict 1542-f4d7b42, follow-up 1; verdict 1542-c5ba7cd, follow-up 3). The judge writes the box too, the one that keeps the layer where it is seen in the frame it goes back to, carried through the turn, the position and the nesting of that frame as the same undo leaves it, and a line's point ends the same way (`replanted` in `packages/doom/src/replant.ts`). It leaves the box alone when nobody wrote it after the step began, since then it is still the frame's own number, and when the parent keeps its frame, as for a reorder. `PutsBackWhereSeen` fails in 53 states without it and in 26 on `main`, its replay `tests/undo-strays-replay.test.ts` fails 32 of 342 edges without it, and `packages/doom/src/invert-replant.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-stray-box.test.ts` and `tests/browser/editor-undo-run-box.spec.ts` fail on `main`. It judges a move only once the moves of the frames above its target are judged, so leaving one move out never drops another that still lands. It leaves out the delete of a frame that would still hold a layer: one whose move it left out, or one someone moved in after the step (`heldFrames`). The deck refuses the writes it leaves out as `missing` or `cycle`, or lands a showing layer where nobody sees it, and `turn` in `packages/live/src/doom-deck.ts` keeps a refused step on top, so one such write made every later Ctrl+Z refused for good (verdict 1494-0ca0e9e, finding 5; verdict 1510-559d9e2, finding 3). A step with nothing left is taken off and answers `nothing`, as Figma does nothing for an undo of an edit to a layer someone else deleted, and the next press reaches the step below. An agent run's undo (`invertRun`) names three kinds of layer it does not put back where they were, so its note names only what a person can find: `left`, layers whose move it left out and that show where they are; `waiting`, layers that do not show once it lands, because they or the frame they go back into are deleted; and `kept`, frames the run made that it keeps because someone changed what is in them. With no write left it answers `left` while any of the three is named, and `nothing` only when none is. Aira's chat and the agent tool use the same notes (`undoStaysNote` in `packages/catalog/src/undo-copy.ts`), beside Undone when something landed and above Undo when nothing did, so neither says "stays where it is" of a layer nowhere on the slide, "refused" of a run that only moved a layer, or "nothing left to undo" of a frame kept for the person's layer (verdict 1514-d30cef9, findings 3, 5 and 7). `specs/undo-deleted-frames/UndoDeletedFrames.tla` fails `NeverLosesALayer` in 54 states with the rule that checked every frame above for a layer that does not show, and `NeverDropsAVisibleEdit` in 44 states when a layer left in place lost its other writes; its replay fails 63 of 469 edges on a78b019's judge, 50 with only the other writes dropped and 13 with only the frames above checked. `NeverStuck` in `specs/undo-steps/UndoSteps.tla` fails in 50 states without the deleted-layer rule. `specs/undo-moves/UndoMoves.tla` fails `NeverLosesAStep` in 48 states with the rule that checked only the frame itself, and `NeverStuck` in 54 when a step refused `cycle` stays on top. `specs/undo-frames/UndoFrames.tla` fails `NeverLosesALayer` in 62 states when every delete lands and in 72 when only a left-out move keeps a frame. `specs/undo-turns/UndoTurns.tla` fails `PutsBackWhereSeen` in 40 states when the layer keeps its turn, as `main` did, `NeverCoversTheirs` in 106 with 759e007's rule and `PutsBackWhereSeen` in 131 with cfcc610's; its replay `tests/undo-turns-replay.test.ts` fails 108 of 489 edges on `main`'s code, 67 on 759e007's and 27 on cfcc610's. `tests/undo-frames-replay.test.ts` fails 27 of 496 edges and `tests/undo-moves-replay.test.ts` 6 of 403 when every frame above is checked for a layer that does not show. `packages/doom/src/apply-edit.test.ts`, `packages/doom/src/invert.test.ts`, `packages/doom/src/standing-layers.test.ts`, `packages/doom/src/invert-run.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-deleted-layer.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-left-in-place.test.ts`, `packages/mcp/src/undo-tool.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-steps.test.ts`, `tests/undo-turns-replay.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-run.test.ts`, `apps/web/src/editor/agent-undo.test.ts`, `apps/web/src/aira/aira-reply.test.tsx`, `tests/browser/editor-agent-undo-notes.spec.ts` and `tests/browser/editor-undo-deleted-layer.spec.ts` fail when a deleted or nested frame holds an undo or a redo, an undo deletes a frame with a layer in it, a deleted layer stays lost under a frame that comes back, a layer left in place keeps a colour, words, size, flip or turn the step gave it or gets a counter-turn against another tab's later turn of its frames, a Ctrl+Z after Undo on an agent run moves or turns a layer the run left in place, or a run's undo names a deleted layer as one that stays where it is, a frame it keeps as nothing left, or a run that only moved a layer as refused, or a move that takes back no delete lands in a deleted frame.

The deck cannot tell a forged `base` from a real undo, because the client sets both the `base` and the `author` of a write, so `takesBackDelete` trusts the client's claim, as every other write it sends. This is accepted: only a hand-made call to `/doom/edit` with the deck owner's own session can send one, and that session can already change the deck any way it likes, so a forged `base` gives it nothing new. Two forged cases are known: an ordinary move with a made-up `base` puts a deleted layer into the deleted frame it was in, and into a deleted frame it was never in, where it stays hidden and comes back attached to another tab's undo (verdict 1533-c85f968, probes f and m). Only undo, redo and run undo set a `base` on a write to `parent`, because `parent` is a `last` write, which `queueEdit` strips of its `base`, and no person edit, paste, offline replay, Aira turn or MCP `edit_deck` sends one; this holds while no shipped client does. The property test in `packages/doom/src/doom.property.test.ts` also deletes a layer before moving it in the same edit, so the deck judges a write on the deck before the edit.
