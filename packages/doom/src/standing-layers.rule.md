# An undo puts a layer back only where it shows and outside itself, and never deletes a frame that still holds a layer, so no frame swallows a layer or holds the steps below it

`onStandingLayers` in `packages/doom/src/standing-layers.ts` is the one judge of what an undo may put back, for a person's step (`invert` in `packages/doom/src/invert.ts`) and for an agent's run (`invertRun` in `packages/doom/src/invert-run.ts`). It leaves out every write to a layer whose parent is `null` unless the same undo puts that layer back, and every move whose frame, or a frame above it, is deleted, or that would put the layer inside itself. It judges a move only once the moves of the frames above its target are judged, so leaving one move out never drops another that still lands. It leaves out the other writes to a layer whose move it left out, since a box is placed in its frame: put back alone, the framed card's old box sat outside its new frame, which clipped it. It leaves out the delete of a frame that would still hold a layer: one whose move it left out, or one someone moved in after the step (`heldFrames`). The deck refuses those writes as `missing` or `cycle`, or lands the layer inside a deleted frame where nobody sees it, and `turn` in `packages/live/src/doom-deck.ts` keeps a refused step on top, so one such write made every later Ctrl+Z refused for good (verdict 1494-0ca0e9e, finding 5; verdict 1510-559d9e2, finding 3). A delete left in hid the layer inside the deleted frame, where no step brings it back: frame a card (Ctrl+Alt+G), another tab deletes a frame above it, and Ctrl+Z deleted the new frame with the card in it (verdict 1514-70f3dd1, finding 1). A step with nothing left is taken off and answers `nothing`, as Figma does nothing for an undo of an edit to a layer someone else deleted, and the next press reaches the step below. A step taken off this way keeps the writes it made, so an older step whose property they wrote last answers `nothing` too, and the layer the other tab moved stays where it shows (verdict 1514-70f3dd1, finding 4). `NeverStuck` in `specs/undo-steps/UndoSteps.tla` fails in 50 states without the deleted-layer rule. `specs/undo-moves/UndoMoves.tla` fails `NeverLosesAStep` in 48 states and `NeverStuck` in 54 with the old rule, which checked only the frame itself and kept a step refused `cycle` on top. `specs/undo-frames/UndoFrames.tla` fails `NeverLosesALayer` in 57 states when every delete lands, 58 with `main`'s rule, and 67 when only a left-out move keeps a frame. `tests/undo-steps-replay.test.ts` fails 48 edges, `tests/undo-moves-replay.test.ts` 41 of 401 on the old `invert`, and `tests/undo-frames-replay.test.ts` 34 of 474 when every delete lands. `packages/doom/src/invert.test.ts`, `packages/doom/src/invert-run.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-deleted-layer.test.ts`, `packages/live/src/doom-undo-steps.test.ts` and `tests/browser/doom-undo-deleted-layer.spec.ts` fail when a deleted or nested frame holds an undo or a redo, or an undo deletes a frame with a layer in it.
