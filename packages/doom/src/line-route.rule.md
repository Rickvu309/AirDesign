# An elbow line between two shapes never crosses either shape, leaving and entering each on its own side

`packages/doom/src/line-route.ts` finds the route by searching only moves that stay out of both shapes' bounds; when the bounds overlap, it searches again around the shapes' true outlines (turned shapes and preset outlines such as a triangle's), leaving each site where it stands, so no case can be forgotten. Two shapes whose facing sides touch and share part of that edge take one plain line along it; sides that sit level but share no edge take the stubbed route. When the two sites meet, the line keeps both ends, a line of no length, and `resolve-slide.ts` draws it with no arrowhead, since it has no direction to point. `packages/doom/src/line-route-sweep.test.ts` routes every side pair between five common presets at gaps from 0 to 60 px, behind, beside and overlapping, and between shapes turned 30, 45 and 90 degrees, and fails on any leg inside either true outline, or a route that leaves or arrives off its side. A pair is exempt only when leaving a site enters the other shape at once. Shapes turned off the quarter may still cross in at most 17 of about 4,800 routes, all with the shapes 10 px apart or less: there a site sits in a narrow diagonal wedge between its own outline and the other shape. In some of these no route of level and upright legs exists at all, since every leg from the site meets one shape or the other. In the rest a stepped route exists, but it turns where the router has no lane: the router turns only on lines through the sites, stubs of 24, 12 or 6 px, and the outlines' corners. There the line goes straight through. The cap only goes down.
