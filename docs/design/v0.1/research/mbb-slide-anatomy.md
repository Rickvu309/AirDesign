# MBB slide anatomy (for AirDesign sample slides)

Sources: Herng Lee guides (From Blah to Boardroom, Demystifying StratOps, Superstar Analyst) plus public descriptions of McKinsey, BCG and Bain slide conventions (deckary.com consulting-slide-standards, umbrex.com quantitative charts guide, slidespeak style notes). No official firm guideline was found; these are the shared conventions.

## Regions, top to bottom
1. Tracker (top left): section names, current one filled. Sticker (top right): "Preliminary", "Illustrative" or "Sample data", outlined.
2. Action title: one to two lines, max about 15 words, states the answer. Same size and position on every slide.
3. Unit line: what the exhibit measures and its unit, small gray ("Revenue bridge, Q3 24 to Q3 25, $M").
4. Body: one exhibit. Direct labels, no legend if labels can sit on the data. Gray for context, one accent for the series that proves the title.
5. Callouts: numbered bubbles or a takeaway box on the number that matters; growth arrows ("+39%") between first and last bars.
6. Footer: rule, numbered footnotes ("1. Projected at current growth rates"), "Source: ...; Halden analysis", page number right.

## Chart rules
- Bars over pies and circles. Sort by value. Zero-based axes.
- Waterfall for bridges (revenue, P&L, value). Bar-in-table for ranked lists. Harvey balls for qualitative ratings. Line for change over time, labeled start and end.
- Colors carry meaning consistently: navy totals, teal accent = the point, mist gray = context, red only for risk.
- No 3D, no gradients, no decorative icons, no animation.

## Content rules from the guides
- Headlines tell the whole story on their own; every chart supports its headline.
- Strong, direct, crisp; commentate, do not label; be grammatical (who does what).
- Order by importance; cut until you cannot; always be selling (every slide asks for something).
- Contextualize numbers (index, benchmark, share of total).
